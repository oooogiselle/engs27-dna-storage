The lighthouse keeper had not spoken to another person in eleven days when the boat appeared on the horizon. At first she took it for a trick of the light, one of the grey shapes the fog liked to make in the early morning, but it held its course and grew larger, and by nine o'clock she could make out a figure standing in the bow with one hand raised against the glare. She put the kettle on, then took it off again, then put it back on. It had been so long since she had needed to decide anything that the small choice felt enormous.

The figure turned out to be a boy of perhaps fifteen, thin and sunburnt, wearing a coat several sizes too large. He tied the boat to the iron ring at the foot of the steps with a knot that would not have held a rowboat in a bathtub, and she went down to retie it before she said a word to him. "You'll lose her like that," she said. "The tide comes in fast here, and it comes in angry."
